# Pluto 2026-11-25: CalcFlow run end to end on a made-up frame, and what the run exposed

Cloud session on branch `CalcFlow`. The user, cautiously skeptical of the whole process, asked for one full pass: a made-up STAAD output with code checks, four welded connections and three anchorage configurations, the DCRs back, the working files, a short README and a screen recording.

> [!info]- What was run
> - **The model:** a made-up frame (21 members, fixed bases, 5 cases, 9 combinations) analysed by the stiffness method in Python and printed in the `.anl` layout of STAAD.Pro 2025, with a simplified H1-1 ratio standing in for the code check. The reader took it with no problems.
> - **The connections:** 8 marks at 26 beam ends on the generic weld and base plate templates (two I-shape welds on one tab, two welds on the rectangular tab, three base plates with their anchor tables typed in one cell each, one circular weld).
> - **The run:** `new`, a sign map and `also Anchors = table B37:D44`, `refresh`, the marks, `check` (26 of 26 ready), `calcs` with LibreOffice (156 copies in 2 minutes: 21 OK, 4 over the limit, 1 invalid), two cells revised, `calcs` again (only the 10 changed calcs ran: 25 OK, 1 invalid).
> - **The check:** the member end forces read from the `.anl` text by a few lines of Python, mapped by hand and run through the templates' reference implementations agree with CalcFlow on 25 of 25 connections to 5e-13, with the same governing case.
> - **The recording:** a virtual screen (Xvfb, a terminal, LibreOffice Calc driven cell by cell) captured with ffmpeg. The demo folder, with the README and the scripts, went to the user; it is not in the repo.

> [!info]- What the run exposed (fixed in `8d06ee0`, with tests)
> - **Harvest chose several governing results.** A row's title named every formula along the row, so the circular weld tab's sweep table (26 cells) and two base plate cross-checks started with Use = Y: the summary would have taken the largest of unrelated numbers. The title now speaks for the first value after the row's labels, and one result is chosen only when exactly one reads as the governing DCR.
> - **Text that starts with a sign.** `-Fz` on Sign Maps, a table that starts with a negative coordinate and `+ ...` in Input cells would be read as formulas by a spreadsheet program. Those cells are text-formatted now, and the word `also` adds given cells to the ones found.
> - **Warnings that meant nothing:** every cell of a table block is guarded by the block's label, and a result at the end of a row of values by the row's title.
> - **`#NAME?`** is said for what it is: the calculating program lacks a function of the template.

> [!info]- Found and not fixed
> - **The circular weld tab needs Excel 365** (`TAKE`, `SORTBY`): LibreOffice gives `#NAME?`, and the calc is reported Invalid. Untested with Excel.
> - **Harvest lists template helper cells off the print area as inputs** (three on the I-shape weld tab). Leaving them blank keeps the template's values; a filter on the print area would remove them.
> - **The sign map is the engineer's decision** and nothing checks it but the engineer. The demo used one map for welds and base plates (template z along the member, y along the web, forces on the connection).

> [!info]- Later the same day: `pipestress` merged in, and a template can take the support reaction
> - **The merge:** 18 commits of `pipestress` (STAAD results in the viewer, a model made in STAAD to the workbook and back, the design in the workbook, the tidy console reports; the `.anl` reader now takes reactions printed in two units). The suite passed on the merged tree before anything else changed.
> - **The user asked** whether a template can be told what forces it takes: member end forces or support reactions. It could not: every template took the end forces of its beam end, the gap the demo had found for anchorages.
> - **Built:** a **Forces** column on the Templates tab, a list of two (Member end forces, Support reaction), per template. A reaction is given in the axes of the beam end the mark sits at and taken like an end force, so the template's sign map serves both. Reactions are converted to the units of the member forces. Refused with the reason: a joint that is not a support, a case in use with no printed reaction. In the library text the step's Slot is `reaction`; `demands.csv` notes the source on every row. See [[vault/calcflow/calcflow-workbook|calcflow-workbook]].
> - **Checked:** on the probe's real `.anl`, the two lone columns (BETA 90) give the reaction as their end forces to the printed digit in all six cases, and the two braced bases (BETA 0) give the column's moments with other forces. On the fixture, the plan's numbers equal the reaction turned by hand, at an I end and at the J end of a column that runs down, with kip-in reactions against kip-ft member forces. On the demo frame (no brace at a base) the 36 base plate demand rows are the same either way, and the workbook made before the column existed checks as before.

> [!info]- The first real output was refused: `PRINT MEMBER FORCES` with a `LIST` (fixed on `pipestress`)
> - **The user's run** stopped with `FORCE_MEMBER_UNKNOWN: member force rows for members that have no incidence: 0`. The file itself was not seen.
> - **Reproduced on the probe** by giving its `PRINT MEMBER FORCES` a member list: the force parser took the command's echoed line (`146. PRINT MEMBER FORCES LIST ...`) for a row of the table. Six or seven numbers in the list give member 0, eight give the line number, three to five or nine give `FORCE_ROW_UNREAD`. The 384 real rows were read each time.
> - **The fix** (`AnlForceParser`, `1739662` on `pipestress`, merged): echoed lines are skipped, and a row of numbers before the table's first member row is reported with its text instead of going to member 0. If the user's file fails for another reason, the message now shows the line.
> - **The other tables** read a list of 3 to 9 numbers on the probe with no change.

> [!info]- Excel repaired the tables of the first real workbook (hardened, cause not confirmed)
> - **The user's report:** Excel opened `CalcFlow.xlsx` with "Repaired Records: Table from /xl/tables/tableN.xml part (Table)" for a long run of tables (photos; about forty). Their templates are not in this repo, and Excel is not here.
> - **Found:** the tables of the demo, of the fixtures and of the whole generic templates folder keep every rule of Excel known here. A template label over 255 characters (a note beside an input) makes a heading Excel cuts, which is such a repair; the schema validator names the limit. Nothing else was found, and whether it is the user's case is not known.
> - **Done:** headings are made what Excel takes at the source and again in the writer (one line, 255 characters, none alike in a wide sense); `XlsxCheck` reads any written workbook against the rules and runs over every workbook the tests write; `excel-check` gives the user the same check on their file, and with the copy Excel repaired, what Excel changed.
> - **To learn from the user's run:** what `excel-check` prints for the workbook Excel repaired.

> [!info]- Three asks after the first real workbook: frozen columns, notes beside the cells, template sheets not picked up
> - **Frozen:** every tab keeps its heading rows and its leading key columns in view (Templates: column A, and so on; Model_Data.xlsx too).
> - **Notes:** a note beside every selected cell, as in the pipe-review workbooks (a data validation's input message): what the cell is and what is expected. `cs/Hints.cs` holds the texts.
> - **Sheets not picked up.** The user's own generic templates showed two real gaps: the inputs of a sheet with fewer than five inputs were on no tab, and the sheets of one calc were split across tabs, so a base plate mark could not reach its anchorage sheet. Now a workbook's calcs come from which sheet reads which, each calc's tab holds the inputs of all its sheets, and a new **Template Sheets** tab lists every sheet with the tab that holds its inputs or the reason it has none. A new workbook has one base plate tab (`Base_Plate_Anchorage`, 69 columns) where it had two; an older workbook keeps its tabs and gains the columns.
> - **Not known:** whether these were the sheets the user missed. Their templates are not here; the Template Sheets tab is where a sheet with no input found shows up.
