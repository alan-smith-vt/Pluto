## Decision

- **No single configuration workbook. The configuration is split by who owns it and how often it changes:**
  - **the calc library:** each locked template's cell map with label guards, and the connection-type recipes;
  - **the connection register:** which detail every connection is;
  - **the analysis package:** generated from STAAD;
  - **the machine config:** paths only.

  [[vault/calcflow/calcflow-overview|calcflow-overview]] has the layers and the roadmap.
- **The library is edited in Excel, and the canonical copy is text.** `library export` writes one sorted CSV per table. The tool runs from the export and refuses a workbook that differs from it.
- **The locked templates are never changed.**
  - Each input is mapped to its cell or defined name, guarded by the text of its label cell, and checked without Excel before Excel opens.
  - A template is pinned by its SHA-256.
  - The record copy is the template plus its inputs only. Its traceability is in the tool's records and a JSON file beside it.
- **The analysis package is the only source of a calc's inputs.** It is written from the STAAD `.anl` ([[vault/calcflow/calcflow-staad-frame-reader|calcflow-staad-frame-reader]]):
  - geometry in inches;
  - end forces exactly as STAAD printed them: member local axes, STAAD's sign, the printed units, combinations as STAAD analysed them.
- **Exact or flagged,** as in [[vault/decisions/2026-11-05-frame-staad|2026-11-05-frame-staad]].
  - What the reader cannot hold exactly is an error, and no package is written.
  - What it keeps but cannot vouch for is a warning, named in the manifest.
- **The branch and the starting code.**
  - CalcFlow starts from Pluto's `pipestress` branch, on branch `CalcFlow`.
  - pipe-review's generic machinery is **copied** into `scripts/calcflow/`, with a provenance line per file: the `.xlsx` reader and writer, CSV and calc state, the COM child stage with deadlines, the Excel helpers, the test harness and lint.
  - Its pipe-specific parts are not copied.

## Why

- **The layers.** A template map changes when a template is revised, a recipe when the company standard does, and an assignment with every model revision. One sheet would make every edit a merge, and would let a project's change alter another project's calcs.
- **Text as the canonical copy.** The library will hold hundreds of cell rows, which engineers maintain in Excel with filters and drop-downs. Running from text gives a reviewable diff, and the tool reads it without Excel.
- **Guards instead of trust.** A locked template revised by someone else can move its cells. A guard turns that into a refusal before a wrong value is written.
- **One package.** It removes the hand carrying the tool exists to remove. A later model source (the pipeline that will build STAAD models from the plant data) only has to write the same package.
- **Copying, not sharing, pipe-review's code.**
  - pipe-review runs on PowerShell 5.1 and 7, and ships to its machines on its own schedule. Pluto is one 5.1 batch.
  - A shared library would couple their releases.
  - The copies are small, carry their source commit, and live in their own namespace (`CalcFlow`), so they cannot collide with Pluto's `XlsxReader` / `XlsxBook`.
